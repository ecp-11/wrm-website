Profile

A flourishing human future depends, in the broadest sense, on two things: our understanding of the natural world and our relationship with it. My work moves between both.

As a geoscientist, my contributions lie in understanding ancient lakes and the rivers that fed them — high-resolution windows into vanished worlds. Lakes record the environments they existed within and the changes those environments endured; because they depend on their rivers, my research traces these large-scale sediment systems to understand the dynamics of a changing world, and what that can tell us about our own.

But data alone has proven a poor instrument for shifting humanity's relationship with the planet. The second branch of my work moves toward that gap — both through research into how art-integrated geoscience curriculum affects students' emotional connection to the material, and through documentary filmmaking that makes complex scientific, ecological, and cultural stories emotionally legible. Character-driven, voiceover-averse, and grounded in the conviction that people protect what they have learned to love.
